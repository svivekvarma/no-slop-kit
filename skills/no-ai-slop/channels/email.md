# Channel: email

Work email, cold outreach, replies, announcements.

The reader is deciding in about four seconds whether to act, defer or archive.
Email slop is mostly padding around a single ask that arrives too late.

## Extra rules

- **The ask goes in the first two sentences**, with the deadline. If there is no ask, say that too ("no action needed, for your awareness").
- **Subject line is a claim, not a topic.** "Ingest worker needs a retry budget by Friday" beats "Ingest worker update".
- **Under 150 words for most email.** Past 250 words it wants to be a doc with a link.
- **No em dashes. No headings. No emoji** beyond a single one in a genuinely light message.
- **Cut the opening pleasantry unless you mean it.** "I hope this email finds you well" is filler. "Thanks for turning the review around so fast" is not, if they did.
- **One thread, one topic.** Two asks in one email means one gets dropped.
- **Name the person who owes the next step.** "Priya, can you confirm by Thursday?" beats "it would be great to get confirmation".
- **Say the bad news in the first paragraph.** Burying it under context reads as evasion.

## Channel-specific slop

| Slop | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| "I hope this email finds you well." | Delete, or replace with a real specific line. |
| "I wanted to reach out regarding..." | "We need X by Friday." |
| "Just circling back on this." | "Following up on the retry budget. Can you confirm by Thursday?" |
| "Per my last email..." | Restate the ask plainly. The passive-aggression is not free. |
| "Please don't hesitate to reach out if you have any questions." | "Questions: ping me on Slack." Or delete. |
| "I'd love to pick your brain." | Say what you want to know and how long it will take. |
| "Looking forward to hearing your thoughts!" | "Can you reply by Thursday?" |
| "Apologies for the delay." then three sentences of excuse | "Sorry this took a week." Then the answer. |

## Keep

Keep the one-line reply. Keep "no, this does not work for us". Keep the
sign-off you always use. A short blunt email is a gift and does not need
softening.
